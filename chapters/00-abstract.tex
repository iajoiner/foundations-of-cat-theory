\chapter*{Abstract}

Let $\cC$ be a category with an initial object. A \emph{$\cC$-cat} is a tuple
$K=(H,T,(L_\alpha)_{\alpha\in A})$ consisting of an initial object $H$ (the
\emph{head}), a terminal object $T$ (the \emph{tail}), and a small family of
objects $L_\alpha$ (the \emph{legs}), connected by the canonical morphisms
$H\to L_\alpha\to T$. We define morphisms of $\cC$-cats by commutative diagrams
and develop the resulting theory systematically: categories of cats, kittens,
and headless, tailless and legless cats; feline functors and natural
transformations; limits and colimits; adjunctions and equivalences; a feline
Yoneda embedding; monoidal and enriched cats; iterated and two-dimensional
cats; feline fibrations; and representation and decomposition theorems. We
introduce the Chloe Condition, Margaret Compatibility and Gideon Decompositions,
and prove a CMG Correspondence Theorem relating them.

Every commutativity condition imposed on a morphism of $\cC$-cats is satisfied
automatically, because each such condition concerns a pair of parallel
morphisms with initial domain or terminal codomain. Consequently the category
$\Fel(\cC)$ of $\cC$-cats is equivalent to the classical category
$\Fam(\cC)$ of small families of objects of $\cC$, that is, to the free
completion of $\cC$ under small coproducts. We make this reduction explicit at
the outset and trace its consequences throughout. All results are proved in
full. None of them is new in substance: each is identified, where possible,
with the standard statement of which it is a special case or a reformulation.
